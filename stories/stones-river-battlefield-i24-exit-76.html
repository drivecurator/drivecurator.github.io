Stones River National Battlefield history, I-24 exit 76 in Murfreesboro

Driving I-24 through Murfreesboro, you pass within a few minutes of one of the bloodiest fields of the Civil War. The National Park Service directs visitors to Exit 76, Medical Center Parkway, then Thompson Lane to the park entrance.

The day after Christmas 1862, Union General William Rosecrans marched his Army of the Cumberland out of Nashville toward Confederate General Braxton Bragg's army at Murfreesboro, about 30 miles south. By the evening of December 30, the two armies were camped along Stones River, in places within 700 yards of each other. Each commander had planned the same move for the next morning: hit the other side's right flank.

Bragg's men moved first. At 6 a.m. on a bitterly cold December 31, General William Hardee's corps slammed into the Union right and drove it back some two and a half miles toward the Nashville Pike and the railroad. The worst fighting came in a patch of woods the soldiers later called "Hell's Half Acre." The Union line bent but held the road and the rails. That night Bragg wired Richmond, "God has granted us a happy New Year."

New Year's Day was quiet. On January 2, around 4 p.m., about 5,000 Confederates under General John C. Breckinridge charged across half a mile of open ground east of the river. Union Captain John Mendenhall had massed nearly 50 cannon there, and their fire broke the attack. Bragg pulled back on January 3.

Of all the major battles of the war, Stones River had the highest percentage of casualties on both sides, nearly 24,700 killed, wounded, or missing out of about 76,400 engaged. The Union victory gave the North control of central Tennessee, and Lincoln later wrote Rosecrans that he had given the country "a hard victory," and that a defeat there was one the nation "could scarcely have lived over."
