Parkers Crossroads battlefield history, I-40 exit 108

If you take Exit 108 on I-40 between Memphis and Nashville, you're standing in the middle of a Civil War battlefield. The interstate and Tennessee Highway 22 meet right where the fighting happened, and the park's walking trails and driving tour stops sit on both sides of the road.

On December 31, 1862, Confederate General Nathan Bedford Forrest was near the end of a two-week raid through West Tennessee, tearing up Union rail lines. Two Union brigades, under Colonel Cyrus Dunham and Colonel John Fuller, were trying to catch him before he could get back across the Tennessee River. Dunham's men found him first, here at a small crossroads where three country roads came together.

Skirmishing started around nine in the morning. Forrest's artillery got the better of the early fight, and Dunham pulled his brigade back about half a mile to redeploy. Forrest's troopers kept working around his flanks until the Union line was pressed from several sides. During a lull, Forrest sent Dunham a demand for unconditional surrender. Dunham refused.

Then the day turned. Fuller's Ohio brigade, coming down from near Clarksburg to the north, heard the fighting, hurried in, and opened fire on the Confederate rear. Forrest was suddenly caught between two Union lines. Asked what to do, he is remembered as answering, "Charge them both ways." His men turned, pushed back Fuller long enough, slipped past Dunham, and withdrew south and across the Tennessee River.

Both sides claimed the win. Forrest left the field and lost guns doing it, but he got his command out of what should have been a trap. The rail damage from his raid took months to repair.

Today the visitor center is a half mile south of I-40, open daily, with paved trails and wayside exhibits telling the story stop by stop.
